Hospital Finance Data Analysis
BUSINESS INSIGHT REPORT
Management Reporting Document
1. Executive Summary
The Hospital Finance Data Analysis Dashboard provides a consolidated view of hospital financial performance, patient volume, claims status, insurance contribution, departmental charges, and Accounts Receivable (AR).
The dashboard reports Total Charges of 19.00M, Total Payments of 10.82M, Outstanding AR of 8.45M, approximately 1.50K patients, and a Collection Rate of 56.15%. The overall business opportunity is to improve collections, reduce outstanding AR, strengthen claims follow-up, and monitor high-performing departments and payers.
2. KPI Business Insights
Total Charges – 19.00M
The hospital generated approximately 19.00M in total patient charges during the selected period. This represents the overall billing volume generated from patient services.
Management Perspective: A high charge volume indicates strong utilization of hospital services. Management should compare charges with actual collections to determine whether billing is being converted efficiently into cash.
Recommended Actions:
•	Monitor charges by department.
•	Identify services generating the highest revenue.
•	Compare billed amounts with payment realization.
•	Review departments with consistently increasing charges.
Total Payment – 10.82M
The hospital collected approximately 10.82M in payments against billed charges.
Management Perspective: The payment volume is significant, but the gap between charges and payments indicates additional revenue remains to be collected.
Recommended Actions:
•	Strengthen payment follow-up.
•	Monitor payer-wise payment performance.
•	Reduce delays in claim processing.
•	Prioritize high-value outstanding accounts.
Outstanding AR – 8.45M
The dashboard shows approximately 8.45M in outstanding Accounts Receivable. AR represents billed amounts that have not yet been collected.
Management Perspective: A high AR balance can create cash-flow pressure. Management should identify whether outstanding amounts are related to insurance delays, claim denials, pending claims, or patient balances.
Recommended Actions:
•	Establish an AR aging review process.
•	Prioritize older outstanding balances.
•	Follow up on delayed insurance claims.
•	Investigate recurring denial reasons.
•	Set collection targets for the revenue-cycle team.
Total Patients – 1.50K
The hospital handled approximately 1,500 patients during the selected period.
Management Perspective: Patient volume provides context for financial results. Management can compare patient volume with charges and payments to understand revenue generated per patient.
Recommended Actions:
•	Track Revenue per Patient = Total Charges ÷ Total Patients.
•	Monitor changes in patient volume and financial performance together.
Collection Rate – 56.15%
The dashboard reports a 56.15% collection rate according to the dashboard calculation.
Management Perspective: Improving collections can directly improve hospital cash flow without necessarily requiring an increase in patient volume.
Recommended Actions:
•	Set monthly collection targets.
•	Monitor collection rate by payer.
•	Reduce claim processing delays.
•	Investigate denied and partially paid claims.
•	Improve patient payment follow-up.
3. Visual-wise Business Insights
Monthly Charges Trend – Sum of Charge by Month
Business Insight: The monthly trend shows fluctuations in hospital charges over time, with noticeable peaks and declines.
Management Perspective: Monthly variation may be associated with changes in patient volume, seasonal demand, department utilization, major procedures, insurance activity, or service capacity.
Recommended Actions:
•	Investigate unusually high or low months.
•	Compare monthly charges with admissions and department activity.
•	Use historical trends for financial forecasting and capacity planning.
Total Charge by Department
Business Insight: The department chart compares charges across Cardiology, Emergency, Pediatrics, Neurology, Oncology, Orthopedics, Radiology and other departments shown in the dashboard. Cardiology and Emergency appear among the stronger contributors.
Management Perspective: High-revenue departments are strategically important because changes in their patient volume or operational capacity can materially affect overall revenue. High charges, however, do not automatically mean high profitability.
Recommended Actions:
•	Monitor high-revenue departments closely.
•	Ensure appropriate staffing and resources.
•	Analyze profitability in addition to charges.
•	Identify opportunities to expand strong services.
•	Review lower-performing departments for improvement opportunities.
Total Charges by Insurance Payer
Business Insight: The donut chart shows the contribution of multiple insurance/payment sources, including Aetna, Blue Cross, Cigna, Medicaid, Medicare and Self-Pay.
Management Perspective: Payer mix is important because payers can differ in reimbursement rates, claim approval rates, payment timelines, denial rates and contract terms. A high-charge payer is not necessarily the best payer from a cash-collection perspective.
Recommended Actions:
•	Create a payer scorecard covering Charges, Claims, Payments, Denials and Outstanding AR.
•	Identify payers with slow payments or high denial rates.
•	Review payer contract and reimbursement performance.
Claims by Status
Business Insight: The claims visual categorizes claims into Denied, Paid, Partial and Pending. Paid claims form the largest portion, while denied, partial and pending claims represent opportunities for revenue recovery.
Management Perspective: Denied and partially paid claims can reduce revenue realization, while pending claims represent cash that has not yet been converted into collections.
Recommended Actions:
•	Paid: close and reconcile.
•	Pending: follow up promptly.
•	Partial: investigate underpayment.
•	Denied: identify root cause and appeal.
•	Track denial rate and denial reasons regularly.
AR Balance by Aging Bucket
Business Insight: The AR aging visual divides outstanding balances into >120, 91–120, 61–90, 31–60 and 0–30 day buckets. The oldest bucket (>120 days) appears to be a major area of concern.
Management Perspective: Older AR is generally more difficult to collect and may eventually require write-offs. The oldest balances should therefore receive management attention.
Recommended Actions:
•	>120-day accounts: escalate immediately.
•	91–120 day accounts: apply intensive follow-up.
•	61–90 day accounts: use regular follow-up.
•	31–60 day accounts: use preventive intervention.
•	0–30 day accounts: maintain normal collection processes.
Average Days in AR by Department
Business Insight: The visual compares average AR days across departments and highlights departments where balances take longer to convert into payments.
Management Perspective: Higher AR days may indicate issues with claim submission, documentation, coding, authorization, denials or payer processing.
Recommended Actions:
•	Review claim submission time.
•	Check documentation and coding accuracy.
•	Analyze denial percentage.
•	Monitor payment turnaround time.
•	Create improvement plans for departments with higher AR days.
4. Dashboard Filters / Slicers
The dashboard provides interactive filters for Month, Claim Status, Insurance Payer and Department. These allow management to move from a high-level financial view to detailed root-cause analysis.
For example, selecting a department can show its financial performance, while selecting Denied claims can focus the review on potential revenue leakage.
Management Benefit: Interactive filtering supports faster decision-making and reduces the need for separate static reports.
5. Overall Management Recommendations
Improve Collection Performance: The 56.15% collection rate highlights an opportunity to improve cash realization.
Reduce Outstanding AR: The 8.45M outstanding AR should be actively managed, particularly older balances.
Reduce Claim Denials: Analyze denied claims by payer, department and denial reason to prevent recurring revenue leakage.
Focus on High-Revenue Departments: Departments generating higher charges should receive appropriate operational and financial attention.
Improve Payer Management: Compare insurance payers based on payment speed, denial rate, reimbursement and outstanding balances.

7. Management Conclusion
The Hospital Finance Dashboard provides management with a comprehensive view of financial and revenue-cycle performance. The hospital generated approximately 19.00M in charges and received 10.82M in payments, while 8.45M remains outstanding in Accounts Receivable. The 56.15% collection rate highlights an opportunity to improve cash realization.
Management should prioritize AR reduction, especially older receivables, strengthen denied and pending claim management, and monitor payer and department-level performance. Improving the revenue-cycle process can help the hospital convert existing billed revenue into cash more efficiently, improve liquidity, and support sustainable financial performance.
Key Management Message: “The primary opportunity is not only to increase hospital revenue, but to improve the conversion of existing charges into timely cash collections.”
